Understanding Taxonomy Structures

Category Types in a Taxonomy

A taxonomy is a hierarchy of categories used to classify and organize content. Categories can be of two types:

  • Group categories—Categories that contain subcategories. For example, the category Soups may include borscht, gazpacho, bouillabaisse, etc.
  • Terminal categories—Categories that do not include any subcategories.

Taxonomies for Topic Classification

Taxonomies are often used for classifying objects within a subject domain. In the DITAToo Web Output Plug-in, taxonomies are primarily used to classify topics. For example, a taxonomy based on product lines can be used to associate topics with specific products. If your content is published with the Classtatic layout (native or customized), users will be able to search for topics by product line.

Taxonomies can be structured in one of the following ways:

  • Simple taxonomy—A strict single-root hierarchy. For example, the Soups taxonomy may be split into vegetarian, fish, and meat soups. Then, vegetarian soups may be further classified as vegetable, fruit, and mushroom soups. Each specific soup, such as maeuntang, will typically belong to one terminal category.
  • Facet taxonomy—A taxonomy composed of several independent hierarchies. For instance, in addition to a classification by ingredients, we can add another hierarchy that includes dietic, elegant, and spicy categories. As a result, maeuntang could be categorized as both a fish soup and a spicy soup. Users can then combine these criteria in search queries.

Inheritance of Categories

An entity within a subject domain, such as a topic, is associated not only with the category it directly belongs to but also with its parent categories. For example, assume the dietic category includes children’s soups and soups for persons with weak stomachs. If alphabet soup is placed into the children’s soups category, it will also be classified under the broader dietic category. This allows users to find topics or items across all relevant levels of classification.
